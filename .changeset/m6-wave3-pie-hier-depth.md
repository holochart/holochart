3D-native pie (E9.12) and depth on treemap and icicle (E8.9), in the full bundle like the rest of 2.5D. `pie`, `treemap` and `icicle` take `depth` (px, a percentage of the slice radius or of a tile's smaller side, or one number per item for height-encoded pies and "city" treemaps), `bevel`, `material`, and a view of their own: `tilt` (±80°, lays the trace back like a pie on a table seen from the front; animatable) and `perspective` (0 parallel … 1 strong). Domain traces share the figure's overlay and have no axes, so each carries its own camera, its domain rect being the tilted plane (`tilt: 0` is the flat trace); `layout.view3d` keeps to cartesian subplots. Slices become sectors (annular for donuts; `pull` explodes in 3D), tiles boxes standing on their parents in terraces; `marker.line` becomes gaps; translucent colors are drawn as the flat trace shows them; labels, leader lines and the path bar are projected onto the tops (depth-tested); hover, click, keyboard focus and drill-down clicks map the pointer through the prism under it (tops and walls), and drill transitions animate in 3D. The trace views are unchanged: the full bundle's wrapper (`extrudedPie`, `extrudedTreemap`, `extrudedIcicle`) captures their primitives and hands them to render's `extrudeDomain`, whose code is in the lazily loaded 2.5D chunk (the 3D add-on for script tags). Treemap and icicle rect data carry each tile's node (`items`), and their primitives always get their data through `update`.
